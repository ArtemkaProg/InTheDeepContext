# InTheDeep --- Visual Reference Catalog

This is a **real-world reference catalog**, not a list of final game
biomes. Use it to study the shapes, textures, scale, and biological
patterns of real seafloor environments. Images are examples, not assets
to copy directly.

> **Usage:** Images below are embedded from their original web-hosted
> sources. If the repository needs offline / local image references
> later, download approved images into `references/`, preserve
> attribution, and change the Markdown image paths to relative local
> paths.

## 1. Abyssal plain and sedimented deep seafloor

![Sea cucumber on an abyssal plain, NOAA Ocean
Exploration](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ex2605-dive09-seacucumber.jpg)

**Source:** [NOAA Ocean Exploration --- What are abyssal
plains?](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/what-are-abyssal-plains/)\
**Credit:** NOAA Ocean Exploration, 2026 Cook Islands ROV Exploration.\
**Study:** Fine sediment, scattered rocks/nodules, organism scale
relative to a broad and quiet seabed. An abyssal plain can contain life
and local details; it need not be empty or perfectly featureless.

Additional reference: [Abyssal Plain Nodules --- NOAA /
USGS](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/okeanos-explorations-ex2104-features-nodule-media-abyssal-nodules/).

## 2. Basalt and pillow lava

## 3. Cold seep and local biological concentration

![Mussels on a carbonate overhang at a cold seep, NOAA Ocean

## 4. Hydrothermal vent field

**Reference page:** [NOAA --- High-Temperature Hydrothermal Vent
Field](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/multimedia/high-temperature-hydrothermal-vent-field/)\
**Background:** [NOAA --- Hydrothermal Vents and
Volcanoes](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/education/hydrothermal-vents-volcanoes/).

Study: chimney shape, mineral deposits, localized plume, relationship
between the vent and surrounding terrain, and organisms adapted to
chemical energy. Do not assume every volcanic area has active vents or
that all vents have the same appearance.

## 5. Submarine canyons and steep terrain

**Reference starting point:** [NOAA Ocean
Exploration](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/).

Search the NOAA site and expedition galleries for: -
`submarine canyon` - `continental slope` - `canyon wall` -
`sediment slump` - `turbidity current`

Study: the cross-section of the valley, layering, slope failures, debris
at the foot of walls, and how visibility and scale change with the ROV's
lights. Add specific image references here after selecting a suitable
geological setting for the game's map.

## 6. Trenches and subduction zones

**Reference starting point:** Search for bathymetry and geological
cross-sections of the [Tonga
Trench](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tonga-Trench) and other
subduction-zone trenches.

Study: - the scale and continuity of the trench; - the relationship
between the trench and adjacent plate margins; - steep slopes and
sediment deposits; - how a real bathymetric map differs from a stylized
narrow crack.

The original screenshot that prompted this concept can be kept
separately as an inspiration image, but should be labelled as a
**schematic geological cross-section**, not a direct depiction of the
playable seafloor.

## 7. Reference use rules

When adding a reference to this catalog, record:

-   what is shown and where it was documented;
-   the original source and photographer / institution credit;
-   the geological or biological feature worth studying;
-   which parts are useful for InTheDeep;
-   what should **not** be copied literally;
-   whether the image is a photograph, scientific visualization,
    diagram, or artistic concept.

Prefer scientific expeditions, research institutions, and documented
imagery for naturalistic references. Concept art can be used for mood
and composition, but should not be treated as evidence of real geology.
